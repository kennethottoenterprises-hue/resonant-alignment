Sanctuary

A monthly letter from Kenneth H. Otto, author of The Law of Resonant Alignment.

A tuned instrument makes a note. An amplified one fills the room.

The book gets you tuned. Staying tuned is a different problem, and it isn't solved by reading something once.

An instrument left alone drifts. Not because anything went wrong — because that is what instruments do. Heat, humidity, time, and ordinary use pull a string off pitch, and the drift is slow enough that you stop hearing it. You only notice when you sit down beside something already in tune.

Sanctuary is that reference pitch, once a month.

It is one letter. Not a digest, not a roundup, not a list of links to things I read this week. One idea, worked through properly, with the Scripture it rests on and something to actually do before the next one arrives.

Eight amplifiers are named in chapter eleven of the book — worship, gratitude, Scripture, community, nature, sound and light, purposeful action, service. Not one of them is digital. That is not nostalgia. It is the reason this letter is written the way it is, and the reason you can have it printed and mailed if you want it that way.

What arrives

The first year works through the twelve ideas the book is built on, one per letter, in order.

  • The opening letter

    Why a letter, and why printed. The argument for analog practice in a life that is being pulled apart by the other kind.

  • Resonance, not attraction

    The physics The Secret got backwards, and what Colossians 1:17 says instead.

  • What you are actually tuned to

    The five-question assessment, worked through one question at a time rather than all at once.

  • Emotions with momentum

    Why the right words arrive two hours after the argument, and what to do in the two hours.

  • And so on, through the twelve

    Resistance and its eleven disguises. Forgiveness as a tuning act. Visualization that isn't fantasy. Love as the highest frequency.

Letters go out at the start of each month. Print copies are mailed from Alberta, so allow a week or two beyond that depending where you are.

On the price

I will be straight about this, because the audience this book found is rightly suspicious of Christian teaching with a price tag on it.

Nine dollars and ninety-seven cents is what the email letter costs because writing one properly takes a week of evenings, and that is a fair number for a month of someone's attention. Nineteen ninety-seven is what the printed one costs because paper, ink, envelope, and postage across a border are real money before I have earned anything, and because folding and stamping them is time I have to protect by keeping that list small.

Nothing here is sold as a spiritual return on investment. You are not buying an outcome. You are paying for a letter.

The book itself is on Amazon at the usual price, and the free guide costs nothing. If money is tight, read those and keep your money.
